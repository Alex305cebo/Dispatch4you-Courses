The top ten U.S. brokers, and how to verify one.

Knowing these companies lets you judge a posting fast. See C.H. Robinson or TQL and you can call without a long check. See an unfamiliar name and you verify carefully.

Number one: C.H. Robinson. The largest broker in the world, $24 billion in annual revenue, 105,000 shippers, 73,000 carriers, operating since 1905. Their Navisphere platform is their own. Payment is stable and the load selection is enormous, but rates often run below market. MC number 128735, days to pay twenty-one, credit A-plus.

Number two: TQL, Total Quality Logistics. Second largest at $8 billion, founded 1997 in Cincinnati, aggressive growth, many young agents. Rates are competitive but days to pay is thirty as standard. One warning: in March 2025 fraudsters used the TQL name for nineteen thousand fake load postings. Always verify the MC number. MC 295557, credit A-plus.

Number three: XPO, or RXO. XPO spun its brokerage into RXO in 2022. About $4 billion, a strong technology platform, good rates on long lanes, working with Fortune 500 shippers. Quick pay available at 1.5 percent. MC 1902590, days to pay twenty-five, credit A-plus.

Number four: Coyote Logistics, owned by UPS, bought in 2015 for $1.8 billion. Three billion in revenue, fast payment at twenty-one days standard, access to UPS freight, good rates and professional agents. One of the best for beginning dispatchers — reliable and steady. MC 568329, credit A-plus.

Number five: Echo Global Logistics. $2.5 billion, founded 2005 in Chicago. Their EchoShip platform has strong analytics, and they are good for LTL and multimodal freight. Rates are average, but payment is stable and service professional. MC 477905, days to pay thirty, credit A.

The remaining five. Landstar System, with a distinctive model built on ten thousand independent agents, $6 billion. Schneider, one of the oldest, founded 1935, paying in fifteen days — one of the best figures in the industry. J.B. Hunt, $12 billion, with J.B. Hunt 360, one of the best digital platforms. GlobalTranz, $2 billion, founded 2003 in Phoenix. And Arrive Logistics, young — founded 2014 — but growing fast, $2 billion, based in Austin, Texas.

Now the most important part: how to verify a broker.

Verification takes five to ten minutes. Those minutes can save you two to five thousand dollars on a single load. The industry loses more than $100 million a year to fraud.

Step zero is the main one: check the broker on your factoring company's website. If you work with a factoring company — OTR Solutions, RTS Financial, Apex Capital, Thunder Funding — each of them has a free broker check tool right on their site. This is the fastest and most practical method there is. Enter the broker's MC number and in thirty seconds you see their credit rating, payment history, days to pay, and a recommendation on whether to work with them.

Why is this the main step? Factoring companies deal with thousands of brokers every day and maintain a current database on each one. They know who pays on time, who is slow, and who does not pay at all. That information updates in real time, and it is far more current than DAT or Carrier411 data.

How it works: go to your factoring company's site, find the section called broker check, credit check or verify broker, enter the MC number, get an instant answer. Most factoring companies also show whether they would buy that invoice. If factoring refuses to buy an invoice from a broker, that is a first-level red flag.
