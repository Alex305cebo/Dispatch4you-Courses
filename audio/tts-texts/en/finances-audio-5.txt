Taxes. The subject most owner-operators and dispatchers ignore until April, when the IRS hands them a bill for fifteen to twenty-five thousand dollars. Do not be that person.

Self-employment tax: 15.3%. If you are an independent dispatcher or an owner-operator, you pay it. Social Security at 12.4 percent plus Medicare at 2.9 percent equals 15.3 percent of net profit. And that is on top of income tax. On $80,000 of net profit, self-employment tax is $12,240, plus federal income tax of roughly $9,000. Total: about $21,300, which is twenty-seven percent of your net profit.

Quarterly estimated taxes. The IRS wants payment four times a year: April 15th, June 15th, September 15th and January 15th. Skip the quarterly payments and you get penalties and interest. The rule: put twenty-five to thirty percent of every check into a separate savings account for taxes. That money already belongs to the IRS.

IFTA, the International Fuel Tax Agreement. A quarterly fuel report for trucks running in more than one state. Deadlines: April 30th, July 31st, October 31st, January 31st. Late penalties run fifty to five hundred dollars per quarter.

Now the deductions that matter. Fuel: one hundred percent. Truck payment and lease: one hundred percent. Insurance: one hundred percent. Maintenance and repairs: one hundred percent. Meals, per diem, at $69 a day for drivers: eighty percent deductible, and you do not need to keep every receipt. Phone, TMS, load board, QuickBooks: one hundred percent. Home office: five dollars per square foot up to 300 feet, which is $1,500 a year.

Cash flow. In trucking you spend money today — fuel, tolls, maintenance — and get paid in thirty to forty-five days. That gap kills businesses. Three ways to close it. First, factoring: money in twenty-four to forty-eight hours for a fee of one to five percent. Second, a cash reserve: keep two to three months of expenses in savings. Third, quick pay from brokers: paid in one to seven days for a fee of one to three percent.

One piece of advice. Hire a CPA who specializes in trucking. It costs five hundred to fifteen hundred a year and pays for itself through deductions alone. A good CPA saves you thousands in taxes every year.
