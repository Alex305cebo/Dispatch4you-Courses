Beyond the TMS, dispatchers use a dozen more specialized tools. Here they are in detail.

GPS tracking and telematics. GPS shows every truck's location in real time, and that gives you several things. An accurate ETA for the broker: you can say "your load arrives in two hours fifteen minutes" instead of "the driver says soon." Route control: you can see the driver is running the right way. Geofencing: automatic alerts when a truck arrives at pickup or delivery. And proof in disputes: GPS shows the driver was on site at exactly eight in the morning. Most ELD devices already include GPS. Standalone trackers cost $15 to $30 a month per truck.

Fuel cards. They give discounts of five to fifty cents a gallon and automatic expense control. The popular ones: WEX, with the widest station network; Comdata, which integrates with TMS systems; EFS for owner-operators; and the RTS fuel card from the factoring company. How it works: the driver fuels with the card, the transaction lands in the system automatically, and you see spending in real time. You can set limits — five hundred dollars a day maximum, diesel only, specific stations only. On a five-truck fleet the savings run about $2,500 a month.

Document tools. DocuSign and HelloSign for electronic signatures on rate cons and contracts, $10 to $25 a month. CamScanner and Adobe Scan for scanning BOLs and PODs straight from a phone, free. Google Drive or Dropbox for storage, organized simply: a folder per load, holding the rate con, BOL, POD and invoice.

One piece of advice worth the whole section: teach your drivers to use CamScanner. Document photo quality improves tenfold, and you stop receiving blurry, unreadable BOLs.

Accounting. QuickBooks is the U.S. small business standard: invoicing, expense tracking, tax reports, reconciliation, $25 to $80 a month. Alternatives: FreshBooks, which is simpler, and Wave, which is completely free for basic features. Many modern TMS platforms already include an accounting module, so check that before buying a separate tool.

AI and automation. In 2025 and 2026, AI is actively changing trucking. ChatGPT handles email templates, broker replies and document drafting. AI load matching powers the rate forecasts in DAT and Truckstop. And automated dispatching from Motive and Samsara assigns loads to drivers based on hours of service, location and preferences. AI will not replace a dispatcher yet, but it already saves one to two hours a day on routine.
