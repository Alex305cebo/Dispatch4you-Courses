What is a freight dispatcher? A freight dispatcher coordinates every part of moving a load, standing as the link between drivers, brokers, shippers and receivers. You are the operations center of any trucking company, and demand for skilled dispatchers stays steady.

The core functions. First, finding loads — that is forty to sixty calls a day. Second, negotiating with brokers, where you pull out an extra ten to thirty cents a mile. Third, planning routes around hours-of-service rules. Fourth, coordinating with drivers in real time. Fifth, handling paperwork: BOL, POD, rate confirmation. Sixth, solving problems, twenty-four seven.

Now, how a dispatcher differs from a broker and from a logistics coordinator. A dispatcher works for one specific carrier, manages their trucks and finds loads for their drivers. Pay runs $44,000 to $90,000 a year, or a commission around five percent. A broker is the middleman between shippers and carriers, holds a broker authority, and takes fifteen to thirty percent of the rate. A logistics coordinator works for the shipper and arranges outbound freight, but does not manage trucks directly.
