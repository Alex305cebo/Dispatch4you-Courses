Paperwork is the legal foundation of every load. The rate confirmation is your contract. The bill of lading proves the freight was picked up. The proof of delivery is the key to getting paid. A mistake in any of them can cost thousands: an unpaid load, an insurance dispute, a lost client. This module teaches you to read, check and handle every document correctly.

Every load carries a chain of paperwork, and each piece has legal weight and a specific job. Skip one link, or get it wrong, and you get delayed payment, a fight with the broker, or lost money. The dispatcher controls that whole chain, from receiving the rate con to sending the invoice.

The main rule: no document, no proof. No proof, no payment. If the driver loses the BOL, or fails to photograph the receiver's signature, or you send the POD late, the broker has every right to delay or refuse payment. Paperwork is your money in paper form.

Here is the chain from booking to payment. Six steps, six documents.

Step one: the rate con. Your legal contract with the broker. It locks the rate, the route, the dates, the equipment type and the payment terms. Verify the broker's MC number through your factoring company before signing — a mismatch means double brokering. This happens before loading.

Step two: the BOL, the bill of lading. The legal receipt for the freight. The driver signs it at pickup. Count the pallets, check the weight, look for damage. Write any discrepancy on the BOL before signing. This happens at pickup.

Step three: transit. The driver is rolling. Track the ETA and tell the broker about delays early. If the driver waits more than two hours at pickup or delivery, record arrival and departure times for detention pay.

Step four: the POD, proof of delivery. Photograph the signed BOL right after unloading — all four corners visible, text readable. Send it to the broker within twenty-four to forty-eight hours. Without a POD, both the broker and the factoring company will refuse to pay.

Step five: the invoice. The bill, listing the load number, route, rate and any extra charges — detention, lumper. Send it in the same email as the POD. The sooner it arrives, the sooner the net 30 clock starts.

Step six: payment. Net 30 means money in thirty days. Quick pay means two to five days, minus two to three percent. Factoring means money within twenty-four hours for a fee of two to five percent, and it is the best option for steady cash flow.

Remember the numbers: six key documents, twenty-four hours to send the POD, three years to keep records, and zero dollars without a POD. No proof of delivery, no payment.
