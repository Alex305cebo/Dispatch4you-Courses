A dispatcher's tech stack is the set of programs and services you use every day. At the center sits the TMS, the transportation management system — software that pulls load management, drivers, paperwork and finances into one place. Around it sit the specialized tools: load boards for finding freight, ELDs for monitoring hours of service, GPS for tracking trucks, factoring for fast payment, and accounting software for the books.

Why does this matter so much? Without a TMS, a dispatcher spends sixty to seventy percent of the day on routine: typing data by hand, hunting for documents, chasing payments, calling drivers to ask where they are. With a TMS, that routine is automated, and seventy percent of your time goes to what actually makes money — finding loads, negotiating, building relationships with brokers.

The income difference is real. A dispatcher without a TMS handles three to five loads a day. A dispatcher with one handles ten to fifteen. That is three times the work in the same hours.

What does the whole stack cost? A minimum setup to start runs $150 to $200 a month: a load board, a basic TMS and a phone. A professional setup runs $300 to $400 a month: a TMS with integrations, load boards, ELD and factoring, full accounting and real analytics.

The main rule: do not buy everything at once. Start with the minimum — a load board and a phone. Add tools as you grow, when each one starts genuinely saving you time. The investment pays for itself with the first extra load per week.

The most common beginner mistake is trying to run ten drivers on nothing but Excel and a phone. That leads to errors, lost documents and delayed payments. Technology is not a luxury here. It is the foundation of professional dispatching.
