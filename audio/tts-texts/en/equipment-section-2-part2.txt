Step deck, also called a drop deck. A lowered platform for tall freight. It has two levels: an upper deck of about 11 feet and a lower deck of 37 to 41 feet. That lower section sits below standard flatbed height, which lets you haul taller loads without special permits. It carries construction equipment, generators, industrial machinery. Dimensions: 48 to 53 feet. Weight up to 43,000 pounds, cargo height up to 10 feet. Rate per mile: two dollars eighty to three dollars eighty.

Power only. Just the tractor, no trailer. The driver arrives with the tractor and hooks up the customer's trailer on site. This works when the shipper has their own equipment — drop trailers. It is popular with Amazon, Walmart and the big retailers. Less wear on your own trailer, but a narrower choice of loads. Weight depends on the trailer. It runs on a drop-and-hook system. Rate per mile: one dollar eighty to two fifty.

Box truck, also called a straight truck. One solid vehicle for local delivery. The body sits on the same frame as the cab, so nothing uncouples. Length runs 16 to 26 feet. It handles local and regional delivery, smaller freight, last-mile work. No CDL required under 26,000 pounds GVWR, which makes it popular for e-commerce and small carriers. Weight up to 26,000 pounds. Rate per mile: two to three dollars.
