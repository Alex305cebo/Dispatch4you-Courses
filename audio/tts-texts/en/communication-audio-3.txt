Twelve phrases a dispatcher uses every single day. Learn them by heart — they cover eighty percent of ordinary situations.

One. "I have a truck available in Dallas." That is your opening line when you call a broker about a load off the board.

Two. "What's your best rate on this?" Use it at the start of the negotiation. Do not name your number first. Let the broker go first.

Three. "Can you send the rate con?" Use it once the rate is agreed. Never take a load without a written rate confirmation.

Four. "Driver picked up, no issues, ETA is four PM." The mandatory check call after pickup.

Five. "Heads up, driver is running thirty minutes late." "Heads up" is the professional way to say "important information coming." Always use it for delays.

Six. "Delivered on time, POD attached." Send it to the broker after every successful delivery.

Seven. "What's the detention policy on this load?" Ask before you sign the rate con. Detention is what the driver gets paid for waiting. If you do not ask, you lose that money.

Eight. "I need $2,800 to make this work." A confident line in a negotiation. Not "I would like" — "I need." That is a different position entirely.

Nine. "Let me check my driver's availability and get back to you." Use it when you need time to think. Never accept a load without checking the driver's hours of service first.

Ten. "Is the load still available?" Always your first question on the call. Good loads get taken in minutes.

Eleven. "I appreciate the offer, but I can't make it work at that rate." A polite, professional no. Better this than taking a load that loses money.

Twelve. "Looking forward to working with you again." Use it at the end of every good deal. Relationships are built out of exactly these small things.
