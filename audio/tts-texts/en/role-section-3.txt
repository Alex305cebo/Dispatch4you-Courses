The dispatcher's duties in detail, with examples from real practice.

Duty one: finding profitable loads. Where do you look? Load boards — DAT Power at $150 a month, Truckstop at $180, 123Loadboard at $100. That is eighty percent of all freight. Direct broker contacts: calling people you have already worked with gets you better rates. Dedicated lanes: steady routes from brokers you trust. And freight matching apps — Uber Freight, Convoy — newer platforms with instant booking.

How to judge a load. Rate: minimum $2.20 to $2.50 a mile for dry van, $2.50 to $2.80 for reefer. Deadhead: no more than fifty to a hundred miles to the pickup. Broker rating: check Carrier411, minimum three and a half stars out of five. Payment terms: quick pay in one to five days at a two to five percent fee, or standard net 30.

Duty two: negotiating with brokers. Forty to sixty calls a day. Tactics: know your minimum rate before you dial. Lead with your advantages — my driver is already in that area, we have an excellent safety rating. Be ready to walk away; decline politely and the broker often calls back in thirty minutes with a better number. Ask about the extras: detention, TONU, fuel advance. And use market data: DAT shows the average on this lane at $2.40.

Duty three: route planning. Tools: Google Maps, free, and PC*Miler at $30 a month, the professional tool for trucks. What to account for: hours-of-service rules — eleven hours driving, fourteen on duty, ten off. Fuel stops every 450 miles. And weather, which adds twenty to thirty percent to your time when it turns bad.

Duty four: coordinating with drivers. Morning check-in at six or seven. ETA updates every two to three hours. Confirmation of arrival and loading status. Check calls on the road. Evening check-in at six or seven.

Duty five: paperwork. You send brokers the W-9, the certificate of insurance, your MC and DOT numbers. You receive the rate confirmation, the BOL at loading, the POD at unloading. Keep every document at least three years.

Duty six: solving problems, around the clock. Driver is running late — call the broker immediately, do not wait. Driver has been waiting four hours at a warehouse — file for detention pay. Breakdown on the road — find the nearest service through Trucker Path. Broker has not paid in sixty days — call every week, and file a complaint with FMCSA if you have to.
