A dispatcher is a communication hub. Every day you deal with drivers, brokers, shippers and receivers. Eighty percent of the problems in trucking come from bad communication: unclear instructions, missed updates, the wrong tone.

There are three audiences, and each needs a different style.

With a driver, talk like a partner. Respectful, clear, aware of what they are dealing with on the road. Your driver is your main asset — no driver, no load, no income. A good driver is worth their weight in gold. Turnover in trucking runs over ninety percent a year, and the dispatchers who keep drivers earn considerably more than the ones who do not.

Rules for talking to drivers. One: give clear instructions. The more detail up front, the fewer calls asking where to go. Two: respect hours of service. Never push a driver to run when they do not have the hours. It is illegal under the coercion rule, with a fine up to $16,000, and it is dangerous. A tired driver is a wreck waiting to happen. Three: check in regularly. Do not wait for the driver to call you with a problem — check status two or three times a day. Four: solve, do not blame. The driver is late for pickup. Not "why are you late," but "okay, let's sort it out, I'm calling the shipper." Five: say thank you. "Great work. I have a good load for you tomorrow." That simple sentence keeps drivers better than money does.

With a broker, keep a professional business tone. They judge you on two things: reliability, do you deliver on time, and communication, do you keep them informed. A broker who does not know where your truck is gets nervous. A nervous broker does not give you the next load.

With shippers and receivers, treat them like customers. Polite, quick, focused on solving the problem. You deal with them less often, but when you do it matters — their impression decides whether you see good freight from them again.

And the rule behind all of it: trucking is a small world. A driver you were rude to tells ten other drivers. A broker you let down does not call again. Reputation takes years to build and one phone call to lose.
