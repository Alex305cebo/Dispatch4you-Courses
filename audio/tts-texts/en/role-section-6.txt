The dispatcher's tools, with current prices and recommendations.

First and most important: the TMS, $50 to $200 a month. The central program for managing freight. Common choices: Axon, McLeod, Tailwind, Rose Rocket. In it you create loads, assign drivers, track delivery status, generate invoices and manage documents.

Second: load boards, $100 to $180 a month. DAT Power at $150 is the most popular. Truckstop at $170. 123Loadboard at $100 as the budget option. Here you search for freight, read market rates and check broker ratings.

Third: a VoIP phone, $10 to $30 a month. Google Voice is free to start, Grasshopper is $26, RingCentral is $30. You need an American phone number to call brokers and drivers.

Fourth: PC*Miler at $30 a month. The professional routing tool for trucks. It accounts for truck-legal routes, tolls, fuel stops and low bridges. Google Maps is free for basic planning.

Fifth: Carrier411 at $30 a month. Checking a broker's rating before you take their load. It shows payment history, claims, and ratings from other carriers. Minimum acceptable: three and a half out of five stars.

Sixth: factoring, two to five percent of the invoice. The factoring company buys your invoice and pays you immediately instead of you waiting thirty days. Common ones: OTR Capital, Triumph, RTS.

The minimum kit to start: a computer or laptop, $300 to $800. Internet, at least 10 megabits, $40 to $60 a month. A phone, Google Voice, free. A load board, DAT Power, $150 a month. Email, Gmail, free. Spreadsheets, Google Sheets, free. That comes to about $120 a month to get going. Add the TMS later, once you are running three to five trucks.
