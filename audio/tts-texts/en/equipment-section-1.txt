Trucking equipment: the basics. The industry sorts trailers clearly by what they are built to do. Enclosed trailers, like dry vans and reefers, protect the freight. Open trailers, like flatbeds and step decks, handle oversized and heavy loads. And specialized equipment, like RGNs, tankers and auto haulers, covers the unusual jobs. Each type has its own limits on weight, dimensions and how it gets loaded.

Federal law caps the combined weight of truck and cargo at 80,000 pounds on interstate highways. With the tractor and trailer weighing around 35,000 pounds, that leaves roughly 45,000 pounds for freight. Going over means a fine of $10,000 or more, and the driver gets put out of service.

Standard trailer dimensions: 53 feet long, a maximum height of 13 feet 6 inches, and a width of 8 feet 6 inches. A dry van holds 26 standard pallets.

Know these numbers. Every dispatcher needs them.
