Accessorials are the additional payments on top of the base rate. Most beginners do not even know they can and should ask for them. Experienced dispatchers earn an extra five hundred to two thousand dollars a month from accessorials alone.

Detention pay: compensation for the driver waiting. The standard is $75 an hour after two hours of free time. If the driver waits four hours at unloading, you collect $150 in detention. Without that clause in the rate confirmation, you get nothing for the wait. Always confirm detention before booking.

Layover: payment for a forced overnight. If the driver has to stay a night because the shipper or receiver delayed them, that is a layover. The standard is $250 to $350 a night, and some brokers pay $400 or more. The key: the layover must not be the driver's fault. If the driver was late, that is their problem. Document the cause of the delay.

TONU, truck ordered not used: payment when the broker or shipper cancels after you have already sent the driver. The standard is $150 to $250, or $350 and up if the driver already arrived at pickup. TONU must be written into the rate confirmation — without that clause, collecting is difficult.

Lumper fee: payment for third-party unloading at the big retail warehouses, Walmart and Costco. Usually $50 to $300. The broker should either reimburse it or warn you in advance. The driver pays on site, gets a receipt, and the broker reimburses. Confirm this before booking: "Is there a lumper fee at delivery?"

Extra stops: if the load has multiple pickup or delivery points — a multi-stop — every additional stop should be paid separately. The standard is $50 to $150 per stop, because it costs the driver time, maneuvering and waiting. Always confirm the number of stops before booking.

Now, checking the rate confirmation. You agreed to $2,500 on the phone, but the rate con says $2,200. Or there is no detention pay, even though you discussed it. If you signed, that is your problem — the broker will simply say the document says $2,200.

What to check. First, the rate matches what you discussed on the phone, and it is all in, including fuel surcharge. If the broker wrote less, do not sign; ask for a correction. Second, detention pay is written in, with the rate — usually $75 an hour — and the free time, usually two hours. Without that clause there is no detention. Third, the TONU clause exists, covering cancellation after dispatch. Without it, if the broker cancels after your driver rolled, you get nothing. The standard is $200 to $350. Fourth, the broker's MC number is verified. Check it through your factoring company. If the MC number on the rate con does not match the one on the load posting, that is double brokering — do not sign.

Always read the rate con before signing. Check the rate, detention, TONU, pickup and delivery dates, and the broker's MC number. Once you sign, it is law, and fixing it afterwards is impossible. Five minutes of checking saves you thousands of dollars and weeks of argument.

Accessorials and a properly checked rate con are your insurance against losing money. Use this on every single load.

Negotiation is a skill that pays in real money — twenty to fifty thousand dollars a year separates those who negotiate from those who do not. Practice the techniques, learn the market, be confident, and your income will grow.
