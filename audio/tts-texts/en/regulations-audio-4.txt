Category five: controlled substances and alcohol, threshold sixty-five percent. A positive drug or alcohol test, refusing a test, or possession. The consequences are the most severe of any category — immediate out of service, loss of CDL, and criminal liability.

Category six: hazardous materials, threshold eighty percent. Violations while hauling dangerous goods — wrong labeling, missing placards, leaks, improper loading. It applies only to hazmat carriers, and the fines are especially high, up to $75,000.

Category seven: driver fitness, threshold eighty percent. Driver qualification — a valid CDL, a current medical certificate, the proper endorsements. An expired CDL or medical card means out of service. The dispatcher should be tracking the expiry dates on driver documents.

Now ELDs, electronic logging devices. An ELD connects to the truck's engine and records driving time automatically. The ELD mandate has been in force since December 2019: every commercial carrier must use a certified device, and paper logs are no longer accepted, except under the short-haul exception or when a device fails.

What it records: engine hours, vehicle movement and GPS coordinates, and driver status — driving, on duty, off duty, sleeper berth — with start and end times for each. The ELD holds the current day plus the previous seven, eight days total. At a roadside inspection the driver must show that data to the DOT officer.

The penalties. No ELD: an out-of-service order plus a fine up to $16,000. Tampering, meaning any attempt to alter the data: up to $16,000 and possible criminal charges. A malfunctioning ELD: the driver may use paper logs for a maximum of eight days, then goes out of service — and must carry eight blank paper log sheets in the cab for exactly that case.

For the dispatcher: you need to read your driver's ELD data. Before booking, ask to see it — show me your ELD, how many hours are left. Many TMS platforms integrate with ELDs and show driver hours in real time. Common devices: Motive, formerly KeepTruckin, Samsara, Omnitracs and EROAD. Cost runs $20 to $45 a month per device including the subscription.

Insurance: types and requirements. Insurance is mandatory in trucking. Without proper coverage a carrier cannot get MC authority, cannot work with brokers, and risks losing everything in a crash. FMCSA sets minimums, but many brokers require more.

Auto liability: $750,000 to $1 million, mandatory. It covers damage to third parties in a crash — property damage, injury, death. The FMCSA minimum is $750,000 for general freight, $1 million for hazmat, and $5 million for certain hazmat classes A and B. Most brokers require $1 million even for ordinary freight. Cost runs $8,000 to $15,000 a year for a new carrier.
