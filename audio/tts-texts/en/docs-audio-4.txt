The paperwork checklist for every load. Seven points, seven required actions.

Point one: get and check the rate con before the driver rolls. Never send a driver without a signed rate confirmation. Verify the rate, route, dates, equipment, detention, TONU, payment terms and the broker's MC number. If anything differs from what you agreed verbally, do not sign — call the broker.

Point two: the driver checks the freight and the BOL at pickup. Count pallets and boxes. Inspect for damage. Compare against the BOL. Write any discrepancy on the BOL. Sign it, photograph it, send you the photo.

Point three: record the times when there are delays. Detention. Timestamped photos of arrival at pickup and at delivery. Photos of loading and unloading starting. Photo of departure. GPS data from the ELD. If the wait passes two hours, notify the broker and document it.

Point four: get the receiver's signature on the BOL at delivery. Signature, plus stamp, plus date, plus time. If the receiver notes damage, record it. If they refuse to sign, the driver does not leave — they call you and the broker.

Point five: photograph every page. That is your POD. Sharp photos in good light, all four corners visible, text readable, signatures clear, each page shot separately. Send it to the dispatcher immediately after delivery.

Point six: send invoice plus POD to the broker or factoring company the same day. One email: the invoice as a PDF, the POD as a PDF with the BOL photos, plus any supporting documents — lumper receipt, detention receipt. The faster it goes out, the faster you are paid. If you factor, send it to the factoring company.

Point seven: file everything in the load's folder. Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive. Structure it by year, month, load number: rate con, BOL, POD, invoice, supporting documents. Keep it at least three years. This is your protection in disputes, audits and insurance claims.

Remember: these seven points are not bureaucracy. They are your money, your protection and your reputation. A dispatcher who works from a checklist never loses money on paperwork.
