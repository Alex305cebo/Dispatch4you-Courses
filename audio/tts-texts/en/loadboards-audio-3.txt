Reading a posting. Every load post carries a set of fields a dispatcher must read and judge in seconds. An experienced dispatcher scans fifty postings in fifteen minutes and knows instantly which ones deserve attention.

Let's go field by field. Origin: Chicago, Illinois, zip 60601. The pickup city and zip — check your deadhead from the driver's current position. Destination: Atlanta, Georgia, zip 30301. The delivery city — think about whether you can find a load back out of that region. Miles: 716. The route distance, which you use to calculate rate per mile. Rate: $1,860 all-in. Divide by 716 miles and you get $2.60 a mile. Equipment: dry van, 53 feet. Make sure your driver has the right trailer. Weight: 38,000 pounds, against a maximum of 45,000 for a standard truck. Pickup: March 27th, eight in the morning to two in the afternoon, Central time. Check your driver's hours against that window. Delivery: March 28th, six in the morning to noon, Eastern time. Broker: XPO Logistics, MC number 123456 — verify that number through FMCSA. Terms: net 30, or quick pay at two percent. Age: posted twenty-three minutes ago. A fresh posting is a good sign.

Now, how to search. Step by step.

Step one: pin the driver's current position — city, state, zip. That is your starting point. Step two: set the search radius. Start at fifty miles; if freight is thin, widen to a hundred or a hundred fifty. Step three: pick the equipment type — dry van, reefer, flatbed — and only the type your driver actually has. Step four: set a destination if the driver wants to head home. Step five: filter by weight and length. Standard dry van, 53 feet, 45,000 pounds maximum. Step six: set the pickup date. If the driver is free today, search today and tomorrow. Step seven: sort by rate per mile, highest first. That puts the most profitable loads on top. Step eight: analyze the top ten. Do not grab the first thing you see — compare the top five or ten on rate per mile, deadhead, backhaul potential, broker reliability and payment terms. Step nine: check the broker. Open their profile, look at the rating, the reviews, the payment history, the MC number. Step ten: decide fast. Load boards run in real time and good freight disappears in minutes. If it fits, call immediately.

Two tips. Save your search templates — if you run popular lanes, save the parameters and stop rebuilding them. And use alerts: set notifications for the lanes and rates you want, and the platform messages you when something matching appears.

Remember, finding freight is a skill. The more you practice, the faster you spot the good ones.
