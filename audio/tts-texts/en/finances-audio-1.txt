Eighty percent of new trucking companies close within their first year. The main reason is not a lack of freight. It is bad financial management.

The average owner-operator grosses about $230,000 a year. After every expense, net profit lands at $50,000 to $80,000 — a margin of twenty-five to thirty percent. The top twenty percent clear $150,000 and up. The bottom thirty percent run at a loss and shut down. The difference is not how many loads they hauled. It is how they managed the money.

Here is how the failure usually goes. They buy a truck for $150,000 on credit. They never calculate cost per mile. They take loads at $1.80 a mile while their costs run $2.00 a mile, so every trip loses money. Six months in, there is nothing left for insurance. At nine months, nothing for repairs. At twelve, bankruptcy. All because they did not know their cost per mile before they started.

Why a dispatcher needs this. Even if you are not an owner-operator, even if you work as an independent dispatcher, you have to understand the carrier's finances. Your job is to find profitable loads. If you do not know the driver's cost per mile, you do not know the minimum rate per mile they need. Financial literacy is what makes you a valuable partner instead of just someone who finds loads.

The profit formula in trucking is simple. Revenue is rate per mile times miles. Expenses are cost per mile times miles. Revenue minus expenses is profit.

It comes down to two numbers. RPM: how much you earn per mile. CPM: how much you spend per mile. If RPM is higher than CPM, you are profitable. If RPM is lower than CPM, you are paying for the privilege of working.

Your job as a dispatcher: push RPM up through negotiation, and keep CPM down through smart planning.
