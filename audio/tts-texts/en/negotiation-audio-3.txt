Techniques six through ten. More advanced, no less effective.

Technique six: negotiate the accessorials. If the broker will not move on the base rate, work on the extras. Detention pay, fifty to a hundred fifty dollars an hour after two hours. Layover, $250 to $400 a night. TONU, $150 to $350 for a cancellation. These add two to five hundred dollars to total revenue.

Technique seven: time pressure. Say: "I need a decision in ten minutes, my driver is ready to roll." Create urgency. If the broker knows you are waiting, they will stall. If they know you take another load in ten minutes, they decide faster. This works especially well Thursday and Friday, when brokers are trying to cover loads before the weekend.

Technique eight: the walk-away. Say: "I appreciate the offer, but I can't make it work at that rate. Call me if anything changes." This is the strongest position there is. If you cannot walk away, you cannot negotiate. In thirty to forty percent of cases the broker calls back within fifteen to thirty minutes with a better rate. The key: know your minimum rate per mile and never go below it.

Technique nine: bundling. Offer to take multiple loads. Say: "If you can give me consistent loads on this lane, I'll take $2.40 instead of $2.60." Brokers value reliable carriers. Trade a discount for volume — give me three loads a week on this lane and I will take slightly less. That creates relationship freight: steady income without hunting the board every day.

Technique ten: timing. Say: "I see this load has been posted for three hours. What's the best you can do now?" The longer a load sits, the more leverage you have. Thursday and Friday after three in the afternoon is the best time to negotiate, because brokers want loads covered before the weekend. Monday morning is the worst — they have plenty of options.

All ten work, but the key is not using them all at once. Pick two or three for the situation. If the broker is aggressive, use DAT data and the walk-away. If they are relaxed, use anchoring and the accessorials. If the load has been sitting, use timing and time pressure.

Write these phrases down. Say them out loud before your calls. The first ten calls will be hard, then it becomes natural. Negotiation is a muscle, and every call is a repetition. Within a month you will negotiate automatically and your income will rise twenty to thirty percent.
