Fraud, scam types, and factoring.

Freight fraud is one of the most serious problems in this industry. According to CargoNet, 2024 was a record year for cargo theft in the U.S. and Canada. The Transportation Intermediaries Association reports that eighty-six percent of brokers have faced attempted fraud. Industry losses exceed $100 million a year, and by some estimates one in eight freight transactions is touched by fraud in some form.

The best defense is your factoring company. If you use factoring and check every broker through their broker check before booking, you cut out ninety percent of fraudsters automatically. Factoring companies do not approve invoices from suspicious brokers, and that is your first filter. With non-recourse factoring, they protect you further: even if the broker never pays, the factoring company carries the loss.

Why is fraud growing? A low barrier to entry — $300 for a broker license. Digitalization, which makes a fake load board profile easy to create. Weak oversight, with more than 17,000 brokers and few inspectors. And financial pressure on small brokers when rates fall. When the market drops, fraud rises, because desperate brokers turn to illegal schemes.

Now the types.

Type one: double brokering. Broker A takes a load from the shipper but, instead of giving it to a carrier directly, passes it to Broker B. Broker B gives it to you. You haul it, deliver it, send the paperwork — and Broker B disappears, paying neither you nor Broker A. Broker A pays only the original carrier under their contract, and that is not you. You are left with nothing. How to protect yourself: the MC number in the load posting must match the MC number on the rate confirmation. If they differ, decline the load.

Type two: identity theft. Fraudsters copy a legitimate broker's MC number, create a fake load board profile with a similar company name, post loads, and when you call, send you a rate confirmation carrying the stolen MC number. You haul the load, deliver it, send documents, and the fraudsters vanish. The real broker knows nothing about the load and owes you nothing. How to protect yourself: call the phone number listed on FMCSA, not the one in the posting. Ask directly: "Do you have a load from Los Angeles to Dallas on April 12th?" If the real broker does not know about it, that is identity theft.

Type three: fake brokers. Fraudsters obtain a legal MC number for $300, post loads, collect money from shippers, never pay carriers, and close the company within three to six months. The signs: the company is under six months old, has no credit score, uses a Gmail or Yahoo address, has a phone number that does not match FMCSA, and has zero reviews. How to protect yourself: do not work with brokers under six months old without factoring, and check through your factoring company — they know the fake ones.

Type four: fraudulent carriers. Criminals posing as carriers who take a load, steal it and sell it. That is the broker's problem, not yours — you are a dispatcher working with a legitimate carrier, and in that scenario the broker is verifying you, not the other way around.

Now factoring. Factoring is selling your unpaid invoices to a factoring company in exchange for fast payment. You deliver the load, send the paperwork, and the broker owes you in thirty days. But you need money now — for fuel, for the driver's pay, for everything else. So you sell the invoice, and the factoring company pays you eighty to ninety-five percent within twenty-four to forty-eight hours, for a fee of one to five percent. When the broker pays thirty days later, the factoring company collects. You already have your money.

Two types. Recourse: if the broker does not pay, the factoring company takes the money back from you. Non-recourse: if the broker does not pay, the factoring company absorbs the loss and you owe nothing. Non-recourse costs more, three to five percent, but it is the best protection against non-payment. Recourse is cheaper, one to three percent, but the risk stays with you.

Common factoring companies: OTR Solutions, RTS Financial, Apex Capital, Thunder Funding, TBS Factoring. All of them offer broker check on their websites. Use it before every booking. It is your main protection.
