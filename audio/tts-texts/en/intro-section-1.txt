The U.S. trucking industry. A 2024 to 2026 overview.

Trucking is a critical artery of the American economy. It moves goods from the people who make them to the people who buy them. In 2024 the industry posted strong numbers despite real economic headwinds.

Start with the key figures. Annual revenue: $906 billion. Freight volume: 11.27 billion tons. Trucks carry 72.7% of all freight in the country by weight. The industry employs 8.4 million people, including 3.58 million drivers. And 95.5% of carriers are small fleets running ten trucks or fewer.

Here is what those numbers actually mean. Trucks dominate logistics: they haul almost three quarters of everything moved in America. The economic contribution is $906 billion a year. Employment reaches 8.4 million jobs. The market structure is small: 95.5% of companies own ten trucks or fewer. And trucking is critical infrastructure. Without trucks, store shelves go empty in three days.

Now the trend. 2024 was a correction after a record 2023, and that is a normal cycle in this business. Revenue came in at $906 billion, down from $1.004 trillion in 2023. That is a drop of 9.8%. Tonnage fell 1.2%. The reasons: the pandemic boom normalized, inflation bit, and interest rates stayed high. The forecast is stabilization, then growth of three to four percent a year starting in 2025.
