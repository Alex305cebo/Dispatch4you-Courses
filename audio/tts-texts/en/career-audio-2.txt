The career path, from beginner to business owner. Trucking dispatch has a clear ladder with four rungs.

Rung one: training. Two to four weeks. You work through the course, set up your tools, prepare your documents. Income: zero. What you invest is time and knowledge.

Rung two: employee dispatcher. Your first twelve months working for a company. Pay runs $35,000 to $55,000 a year. The upside is steady income, real experience and mentorship. The downside is a hard ceiling and somebody else's rules.

Rung three: independent dispatcher. You work for yourself and take a percentage of every load. At five to seven percent of the freight, with five trucks averaging $8,000 a month each, you earn $2,000 to $2,800 a month. With ten trucks, $4,000 to $5,600 a month. That is $50,000 to $120,000 a year. Most people reach this after six to twenty-four months of experience.

Rung four: dispatch company owner. You have a team of dispatchers, systems, and scale — fifteen to fifty trucks under service. Income runs $100,000 to $300,000 a year and up. It takes one to three years of active work and building real processes.

Most successful dispatchers walk this path in order. Do not skip a rung. Each one gives you experience you will need on the next.
