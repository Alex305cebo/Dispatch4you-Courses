How to find your first clients, and what to put on your resume.

The most common question from beginners: where do I find drivers? The answer is everywhere owner-operators are. They are looking for a good dispatcher just as actively as you are looking for clients.

The best channels. Facebook groups — free and the most effective. Owner Operator Land, Trucking Jobs and Loads, Truck Dispatchers Network. The strategy matters here. Do not post "I am a dispatcher, hire me." Instead, answer questions, share something useful, show what you know. After a week of that, owner-operators start messaging you.

Instagram and TikTok — content marketing. Short videos: how to find a load at three dollars a mile, three mistakes new owner-operators make. One video that takes off brings ten to twenty inquiries.

The free trial week is your best converter. Say it plainly: give me a week for free, I will find you loads, you see the result. After a good trial week, ninety percent of carriers stay.

Referrals are the best channel once you are running. One happy carrier tells three to five others. Ask for referrals actively.

Now, skills for your resume. Working load boards: DAT and Truckstop. Negotiating with brokers. Monitoring HOS and ELD. Working in a TMS: Motive, Samsara, Rose Rocket. Knowledge of FMCSA regulations. Document handling: rate confirmations, bills of lading, proofs of delivery and invoices.

And a final word. You have finished the entire course — fifteen modules, from how the industry works to managing your money. You now know what the job takes.

One thing is left: act. Knowledge without action is worth nothing. Start today.
