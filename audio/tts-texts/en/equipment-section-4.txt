What a dispatcher needs to know about equipment.

Matching the equipment to the load is money. Send a dry van to freight that needs a flatbed and the driver shows up and cannot load. You lose the time, you owe a TONU, and you damage the relationship with the broker. Send a reefer to ordinary dry freight and the driver earns less, because a reefer costs more to run but the rate pays like a van.

Rates follow equipment type. Flatbed and reefer pay twenty to fifty percent more than dry van. The reasons are simple: fewer trucks available, harder work — tarping, temperature control — and higher operating costs. So if your driver is on a flatbed, look for flatbed freight, not van freight. Otherwise you are using a Ferrari to deliver pizza.

Equipment is seasonal. Reefer peaks in spring and summer, the produce season out of Florida and California. Flatbed peaks spring through fall, the construction season. Hopper peaks in fall, at harvest. Dry van stays steady year round and peaks in the fourth quarter with holiday freight. Plan your drivers around that.

And weight limits change by state. The federal limit is 80,000 pounds, but some states allow more with permits. Michigan, for example, allows up to 164,000 pounds on certain roads. Other states apply bridge formula restrictions. Always check the weight limits for the specific route before you book.
