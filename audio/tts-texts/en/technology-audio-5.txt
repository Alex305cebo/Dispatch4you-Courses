You do not need to buy everything at once. Here is a concrete plan for the tech stack, from start to scale.

Months one and two: the starter pack, $150 to $200 a month. Required: a load board — DAT at $45, or 123Loadboard at $29. A phone with an unlimited plan, $50 to $70. Google Workspace at $6, which covers email, Drive and Docs. WhatsApp, free, for talking to drivers. CamScanner, free, for scanning documents. Factoring at two to three percent of the invoice, with no fixed fee. At this stage you do not need a TMS — use Google Sheets to track loads. That works fine with one or two drivers.

Months three through six: the professional pack, $300 to $400 a month. Add a TMS: Axon at $50 to $80, or TruckSmarter at $40. Add a second load board, Truckstop at $39. Add ELDs for drivers, Motive at $25 a month per truck. Add a fuel card, free but saving you money on every fill. Add QuickBooks at $25 for the books. At this stage you are handling five to ten loads a day, and the TMS starts genuinely paying for itself.

Month six and beyond: the scale pack, about $580 a month. Upgrade the TMS to Rose Rocket at $120 or Motive TMS at $150 to $300. Add DAT Power at $150 for full market analytics. Add Samsara GPS with dash cams, $35 to $50 per truck. Add DocuSign at $10. Add a CPA at about $125 a month. At this stage you run five to fifteen trucks, and your technology is your main competitive advantage.

The governing principle for managing your stack: every tool must either save you time or increase your income. If a tool does neither, cancel it. Review the whole set every three months and ask yourself honestly — which of these do I actually use every day, and which just charge my card?

The takeaway. A dispatcher without technology is a dispatcher working against themselves. Tools do not replace professionalism; they amplify it. Start with the minimum, grow deliberately, and your tech stack becomes your biggest advantage in this business.
