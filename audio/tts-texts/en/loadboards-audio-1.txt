What is a load board and how does it work? A load board is an online marketplace connecting people who need freight moved with people who have trucks. Picture a stock exchange, except instead of shares, loads are traded. New postings appear every second, and the dispatcher's job is to find the best one for their drivers.

Here is how the ecosystem works. The shipper — say a Coca-Cola plant — wants 40,000 pounds of beverages moved from Atlanta to Miami. They go to a broker. The broker posts that load on a board with the route, equipment type, weight, dates and rate. The dispatcher sees the posting, judges whether it is profitable, calls the broker, negotiates a better price and books it for their driver. After delivery, the broker pays the carrier.

Why is the load board the main tool? Without one, a dispatcher hunts for freight by hand — calling dozens of brokers, checking email, waiting for offers. With a board you see more than five hundred thousand loads in real time, filtered by route, equipment, rate and dozens of other parameters. It is Google for freight. You cannot work without it.

Who posts the loads? Mostly brokers, eighty to eighty-five percent of everything on the board. The remaining fifteen to twenty percent comes from direct shippers and 3PL companies, third-party logistics. Freight from direct shippers is usually better — higher rates, more reliable payment, fewer problems — but it is harder to find, because those shippers often work on contracts and never post on open boards.

Two kinds of freight. Spot market: one-off loads, which is what you see on the board, with rates that move daily on supply and demand. And contract freight: long-term agreements at fixed rates. The spot market is more volatile, but in peak seasons it pays more. About twenty-five percent of all U.S. freight moves through the spot market.

The life of a load. The broker posts it. Dispatchers see it and start calling. The broker picks the best carrier and sends a rate confirmation. The driver picks up, delivers, sends the POD, proof of delivery. The broker pays. The whole cycle, from posting to payment, takes fifteen to forty-five days depending on the broker's terms.
