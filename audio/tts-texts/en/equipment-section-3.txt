Specialized equipment. Beyond the main six there is equipment built for unusual freight. You see these loads less often, but they pay considerably more, because there is less competition and the work requires real specialization.

Conestoga — a rolling tarp system. It is a flatbed with a retractable curtain, sometimes called a curtain side. It combines the advantage of a flatbed, loading from the side and the top, with the advantage of a van, protection from weather. Use it for freight that needs covering but cannot be loaded through rear doors. Rates run fifteen to twenty-five percent above standard flatbed.

Double drop, or RGN. RGN stands for removable gooseneck: the front of the trailer detaches, so equipment can be driven right onto the deck. It carries construction machinery — excavators, bulldozers — and heavy industrial equipment. Capacity reaches 150,000 pounds and beyond with permits. Rates run five to fifteen dollars a mile.

Tanker, for liquid freight. A cylindrical tank hauling fuel, chemicals, milk, water, edible oils. It requires a tanker endorsement on the CDL, and often a hazmat endorsement too. The cleaning rules between loads are strict. Rates are high, because of the specialization and the risk.

Auto hauler, for vehicles. A purpose-built trailer carrying seven to ten cars. Dealers use it, auctions like Copart and IAAI use it, and so do people relocating. Loading takes real skill. Rates run one to two dollars a mile per car, so with eight to ten cars that is eight to twenty dollars a mile in total.

Hot shot — a pickup with a trailer. A one-ton pickup, a Ford F-350 or a Ram 3500, pulling a 40-foot gooseneck trailer. It handles urgent and smaller freight: oilfield parts, building materials, equipment. No CDL required under 26,000 pounds GVWR, which makes it a low barrier to entry and popular with newcomers.

Hopper, or grain trailer, for bulk freight. It hauls grain, corn, soybeans, sand, gravel. Loading from the top, unloading through hatches underneath. Demand is seasonal: during harvest, September through November, rates spike. Off season, the freight thins out.
