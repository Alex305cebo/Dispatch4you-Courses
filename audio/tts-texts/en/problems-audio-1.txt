In trucking, problems are not the exception. They are the norm. Breakdowns, delays, damaged freight, non-payment, fights with drivers and brokers — all of it happens regularly. A professional dispatcher is not the one with no problems. It is the one who solves them fast and keeps the losses small.

According to FreightWaves, the average fleet loses $560 per truck per week to unpaid detention. That is $28,000 a year on a single truck. Owner-operators lose $5,000 to $12,000 a year to bad paperwork and slow dispatcher response. Most of those losses are preventable.

Here is the core principle: your reaction speed decides the size of the loss. A breakdown is a fact. But did you notify the broker in the first fifteen minutes? Did you line up a backup plan? Did you document everything for the claim? The difference between losing zero dollars and losing five thousand is speed and system.

Problems come in three levels. Red is critical: a breakdown, an accident, cargo theft. Handle it right now, everything else waits. Yellow is serious: detention, a late delivery, a dispute with a broker. Handle it within one to two hours. Green is routine: missing paperwork, a small delay, a scheduling problem. Handle it within the day. Sorting a problem into the right level quickly is a core skill.

Why problems are unavoidable. Trucking is a live system with thousands of variables. Weather changes. Trucks break, and the average U.S. truck is twelve years old. Shippers run late loading. Receivers close the dock early. Roads shut down after a wreck. You cannot control any of that. You can control your response to it.

Now the cost of doing nothing. Two hours of detention: $150. Eight hours of detention, because nobody called the warehouse: $600, plus the next load you did not take, another $2,000. Cargo damage with no photos, claim denied by the insurer: five to fifty thousand dollars out of your pocket. A broker who has not paid in thirty days, and you wait: at ninety days that broker is bankrupt and you get nothing.

Move fast.
