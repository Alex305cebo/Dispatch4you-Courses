Now a real call, start to finish.

The setup: a load from Chicago to Atlanta, dry van, posted on DAT. 716 miles. The lane average is $2.45 a mile. The posting is fresh, fifteen minutes old.

You open: "Good morning, this is Alex with Swift Dispatch, MC number 1234567. I'm calling about your Chicago to Atlanta dry van load posted on DAT." You introduced yourself, gave your MC number, named the specific load. Professional.

The broker answers: "Hey Alex, yes, that load is still available. Picking up tomorrow at 8 AM, delivering next day by noon, 38,000 pounds. What are you looking for on the rate?"

You reply: "I've got a truck available in Chicago right now. Based on what I'm seeing on DAT, this lane is averaging about $2.45 per mile this week. I need $2.65 to make this work for my driver." Your real target is $2.45, but you opened above it.

The broker: "That's a bit high for us. I can do $2.30 per mile, that's $1,647 all in."

You: "I appreciate that, but $2.30 is below the market average. Can you come up to $2.50? That's fair for both of us, and I can have my driver there first thing tomorrow." You declined the first offer, proposed a middle number, and added value — the driver is ready.

The broker: "Let me check. I can do $2.45, that's the best I can do on this one."

You: "$2.45 works. Can you also include detention pay at $75 an hour after two hours?" You accepted your target rate and immediately asked for detention — extra revenue on top.

The result: $2.45 a mile, the market average, plus detention pay. Without negotiating you would have taken $2.30. The difference is $107 on a single load.

Now the beginner mistakes. Each one costs real money.

Mistake one: accepting the first offer. The beginner hears $2,200 and agrees immediately, afraid of losing the load. But the first offer is the broker's opening position, not their final one. In ninety percent of cases they will add one to three hundred dollars. The fix: always ask "can you do any better?" — even when the rate already looks fine.

Mistake two: not knowing market rates. Without DAT or Truckstop data you are negotiating blind. The broker says "two dollars a mile is a good rate," and you believe it, when the lane actually averages $2.60. That is $430 lost on one load. The fix: check DAT RateView before every call. Five minutes of research is worth two to five hundred dollars.

Mistake three: showing desperation. "I really need this load, my driver is sitting empty." That is the worst thing you can say. The broker instantly knows you are weak and will not move on price. The fix: never let on that you need this particular load. Always create the impression that you have alternatives.

Avoid these three and your income rises twenty to thirty percent with no additional effort.
