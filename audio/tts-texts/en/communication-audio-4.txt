Conflict is part of this job. The driver is angry about a bad load. The broker is unhappy about a late delivery. The shipper is complaining about damage. Your job is not to win the argument. Your job is to solve the problem.

Five rules for de-escalation.

One: listen without interrupting. When someone is angry, they need to say their piece. Give them thirty to sixty seconds. Do not cut in. Do not defend yourself. Just listen. When they finish, say: "I understand your frustration, let me see what I can do." That alone drops the temperature.

Two: acknowledge the problem. Do not deny it, do not minimize it. "You're right, this shouldn't have happened" is a powerful sentence. It does not mean you are at fault. It means you understand the situation. People want to be heard.

Three: offer a solution, not an excuse. Not "it's not my fault, it's traffic." Instead: "here's what I'm doing right now — I'm calling the receiver and moving the appointment back two hours." A concrete action, a concrete fix. Excuses do not help anyone.

Four: never raise your voice. If the other person is shouting, speak quieter and slower. This is a real psychological effect — people unconsciously match your tone. Shouting back escalates. Calm against shouting de-escalates. Whoever stays calm controls the conversation.

Five: follow up after it is resolved. An hour later, or the next day, write: "Just checking in, is everything resolved? Let me know if you need anything else." It shows you actually care, and it locks in a good impression after a bad moment.

Here is a real example. The driver calls in a panic: "The dock was closed when I arrived. I've been waiting three hours." The wrong response is "why didn't you call me earlier?" The right response: "Mike, I'm so sorry about that. Are you okay? I'm calling the broker right now and I'll get you detention pay for every hour you waited. Take photos of the closed dock with a timestamp."

The takeaway. Communication is not just words. It is how you manage relationships. A dispatcher who can talk keeps drivers, gets the better loads from brokers, and builds a reputation in this industry.
