Cargo insurance: $100,000 and up, mandatory. It covers the value of the freight if it is damaged, stolen or lost. The FMCSA minimum is $100,000 — but if you haul electronics worth $500,000 a trailer, that minimum covers nothing. Many brokers require $250,000 to $500,000. Cost runs $1,500 to $3,000 a year. Important: check the exclusions. Some policies do not cover reefer breakdown.

Physical damage: not mandatory, but important. It covers repair or replacement of your own truck after a crash, fire, theft or vandalism. FMCSA does not require it, but a leasing company will if the truck is financed. Two types: collision, for crashes, and comprehensive, for everything else. Cost runs three to five percent of the truck's value per year.

General liability: recommended. It covers ordinary business risk — injury on your premises, errors in paperwork, client claims. Not required by FMCSA, but many brokers and shippers ask for it. Cost runs $500 to $2,000 a year, and it is recommended for dispatch companies.

Workers' compensation: mandatory in most states if you have employees. It covers medical costs and lost income from workplace injury. Owner-operators are usually exempt, though some states require it even for them.

Now the fines, and they are serious. A single violation can cost from $1,000 to $75,000. Beyond the money: out-of-service orders that stop the driver or the truck, CSA points that stay two years, rising insurance, lost clients, and in extreme cases criminal liability.

Hours-of-service violation: $1,000 to $16,000, out of service, CSA points, and a repeat can cost the CDL. ELD tampering: $1,000 to $16,000, immediate out of service, possible criminal case. Coercion, pressuring a driver: $16,000, a fine against the dispatch company or broker, and an FMCSA investigation. Operating without authority: $25,000 and up, truck impoundment, criminal liability. No insurance: $16,000 and up, immediate shutdown, loss of MC authority. Overweight: one to ten dollars per pound over the limit depending on the state — five thousand pounds over can mean five to fifty thousand dollars. Drug or alcohol violation: $2,500 to $25,000, loss of CDL, criminal liability, out of service. Hazmat violation: $500 to $75,000 depending on severity, and a leak means a criminal case. Failed inspection: varies, and the truck or driver cannot move until it is fixed, plus CSA points. Falsified records: $16,000, criminal liability, and permanent loss of the CDL.

Remember: the dispatcher carries direct responsibility for compliance. Check your driver's hours before every single booking. Knowing these rules is not theory — it is protection for your business and safety for your drivers.
