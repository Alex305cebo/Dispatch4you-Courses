The skills and qualities a dispatcher needs. This job takes an unusual combination of technical skill and personal temperament, and top dispatchers earn forty to sixty percent more precisely because of it.

Technical skills. First, working in a TMS. The transportation management system is your central tool. Common ones: Axon at $50 to $150 a month, McLeod at $120, Tailwind at $80, Rose Rocket at $100. You need to create and edit loads, assign drivers, track delivery status in real time, and generate invoices and reports. Learning curve: one to two weeks to a working level.

Second, load boards. DAT, Truckstop, and the boards run by large brokers. These are the online marketplaces where brokers post available freight, and eighty percent of all loads live there. You need to use the search filters efficiently, judge a load's profitability fast, check broker ratings, and read market rates by lane. Learning curve: two to three weeks of practice.

Third, U.S. geography. You need all fifty states, the top hundred cities, and the main interstates: I-95 on the East Coast, I-5 on the West, I-10 across the South, I-70 across the North. Four time zones: Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific. And the hot markets where freight is always moving: Texas, California, Illinois, Georgia, Florida.

Fourth, regulations. DOT and FMCSA rules. Hours of service: eleven hours driving, fourteen on duty, ten off, seventy hours a week. Weight limit: 80,000 pounds maximum. The ELD mandate, which makes electronic logs required. CSA scores, the safety rating system for carriers. And insurance minimums: $750,000 in liability.

Now the personal qualities. Communication, ten out of ten in importance — eighty percent of this job is talking to people, forty to sixty broker calls and ten to twenty driver calls every day. Stress tolerance, nine out of ten — sixty to seventy percent of loads have some kind of problem. Multitasking, nine out of ten — you run five to ten trucks at once, each with its own deadlines and its own trouble. Organization, eight out of ten — 250 to 500 documents a month pass through you. Being proactive, eight out of ten — the best dispatchers see problems coming before they arrive. And negotiation, nine out of ten — every ten cents a mile is another eighty to a hundred dollars on the load.
