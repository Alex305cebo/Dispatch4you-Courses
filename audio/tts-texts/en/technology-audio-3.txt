The TMS, transportation management system. Software that brings every part of a dispatcher's job into one interface. If you work without one, you lose money every day.

What a good TMS does. First, the dispatch board: every truck on one screen — where they are, what they are hauling, what their hours of service look like. Second, load management: the full cycle from receiving the rate con to invoicing and getting paid. Third, driver management: hours of service, location, and each driver's documents. Fourth, invoicing: invoices generated automatically from the load data. Fifth, reporting: profit by lane, by driver, by broker.

Integrations are what make a TMS genuinely powerful. Integration with load boards pulls loads from DAT and Truckstop automatically. Integration with the ELD updates driver hours in real time inside the TMS. Rate cons and invoices generate themselves. GPS tracking shows on a map right in the interface. A mobile app lets drivers upload PODs and update status from their phone. And factoring integration sends invoices with one click.

How to choose a TMS for your fleet size. For one to three trucks, something simple and cheap: Trucking Office at $30 to $50, TruckSmarter at $40, Axon at $50 to $80. For three to fifteen trucks, mid-tier with full integrations: Rose Rocket at $120, Motive TMS at $150 to $300. For fifteen trucks and up, enterprise systems: McLeod, TMW, Prophesy, starting around $500.

The main rule when choosing: always take the trial first. Most companies offer seven to fourteen days free. Use that period fully — load real data, try every feature.

And check three things before you commit. One: is there a mobile app for drivers? Without it, your driver cannot send PODs from the phone. Two: does it integrate with your load board and your ELD? Without integration you will type the same data twice. Three: how good is support? Technical problems always happen. Support should answer in minutes, not days.
