Pro tips from experienced dispatchers.

Tip one: build relationships with brokers. When you find a good one — reliable, honest rates — work with them regularly. They start offering you loads before those loads hit the board, and that is a real advantage.

Tip two: learn the seasonal patterns. Summer brings produce and building materials. Fall brings harvest and food. Winter brings holiday retail. Spring brings garden goods. Knowing the pattern helps you plan.

Tip three: work several platforms. Do not limit yourself to one board. DAT, Truckstop, 123Loadboard, Convoy, Uber Freight. More sources, more options.

Tip four: plan chains, not single loads. Atlanta to Chicago, Chicago to Dallas, Dallas back to Atlanta. Three loads, no deadhead, maximum profit.

Tip five: pay for the premium tier. Basic board access is limited. Premium gives you rate checking, alerts, priority access and analytics, and it pays for itself on the first load.

Tip six: keep your own database. Record every load — lane, rate, broker, date, problems. In three to six months you will have genuinely valuable knowledge about which lanes and which brokers are worth your time.

Tip seven: learn from the bad ones. Took a bad load? Work out what went wrong, which red flags you missed, and how to avoid it next time.

Tip eight: be fast, but do not rush. Speed matters, good freight goes quickly. But do not book without checking the details. One minute of verification saves hours of trouble.

Tip nine: develop a specialty. If you are good with a particular freight type or region, lean into it. Specialization brings expertise and better rates.

Tip ten: never stop learning. The market shifts, new platforms appear, rules change, opportunities open. Read the forums, watch the webinars, talk to other dispatchers.

To close. Load boards are the heart of this industry, and modern dispatching is impossible without them. You now know what a board is, how it works, which platforms exist, how to read a posting, how to search, how to judge rates, which red flags to avoid and which habits to build.

Your job now is to use it. Register on a platform — 123Loadboard if the budget is tight, DAT straight away if you are ready to invest. Spend an hour a day reading postings, checking rates, calling brokers. In a month you will feel confident. In three, you will be a professional.

And remember: a successful dispatcher is not the one who finds any load. It is the one who finds the right load, at the right time, at the right price.

Good luck out there.
