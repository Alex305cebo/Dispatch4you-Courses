Freight hubs are the cities with the highest volume passing through them. Knowing the hubs helps you find loads fast and plan routes with minimal deadhead. If your driver is near a hub, there is always freight.

Number one: Chicago, Illinois. The largest transport hub in the country, where I-90, I-94, I-80, I-55 and I-57 meet. More intermodal freight passes through Chicago than any other city, and the warehouses of the biggest retailers and distributors sit here.

Number two: Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas. The second largest freight hub, strategically placed between the coasts. An enormous concentration of distribution centers, with loads available in every direction nearly year round.

Number three: Atlanta, Georgia. The main hub of the Southeast, where I-75, I-85 and I-20 cross. Hartsfield-Jackson, the busiest airport in the world, generates air freight, and Home Depot, UPS and Coca-Cola are all based here.

Number four: Los Angeles, California. The ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach handle forty percent of U.S. imports, and container freight spreads from here across the country. Outbound rates are high; inbound rates are among the lowest anywhere, because everyone wants to leave California.

Number five: Memphis, Tennessee. Home of FedEx and the largest cargo airport in the world, sitting where I-40 and I-55 cross. An excellent point for regrouping routes.

Number six: Indianapolis, Indiana. The state motto is "Crossroads of America," and it fits — I-65, I-69, I-70 and I-74 all meet here. One of the most convenient hubs for planning a route in any direction.

Number seven: Houston, Texas. The center of the oil and gas industry. The Port of Houston is the second largest in the United States. Chemicals, oilfield equipment, building materials — and hazmat freight is a common specialization here.

Number eight: Columbus, Ohio. One of the largest warehouse markets in the country. Amazon, Walmart and Target all run major distribution centers here, with access to sixty percent of the U.S. population within 600 miles.

Number nine: Laredo, Texas. Forty percent of all U.S.-Mexico trade crosses here. Cross-border freight is a growing segment: goods move north out of Mexico and American products move south. The specifics here are customs clearance and FAST cards.

Number ten: Savannah, Georgia. The fastest growing port on the East Coast. A deepwater port taking the largest container ships, growing fifteen percent a year, with freight distributing across the Southeast and Midwest.
