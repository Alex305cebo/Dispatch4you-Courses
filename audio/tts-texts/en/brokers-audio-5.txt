The carrier packet, and building relationships.

A carrier packet is the set of documents a broker requires before giving you your first load. It is standard procedure for every new carrier. Prepare it in advance and keep it in the cloud, Google Drive or Dropbox, so you can send it in two minutes when asked.

What goes in it. First, the W-9 — the IRS tax form with your EIN, your employer identification number, or your SSN. The broker uses it for IRS reporting. Download the blank at irs.gov, fill it in, save it as a PDF. Send it only to verified brokers: a W-9 carries sensitive information.

Second, the certificate of insurance, the COI. Proof of your coverage: auto liability at $1 million minimum, cargo insurance at $100,000 minimum, and general liability. Request the COI from your insurance company — they issue it free. Some brokers ask to be listed as additional insured or certificate holder, which is standard practice; ask your insurer to add them.

Third, your MC authority, a copy of your operating authority from FMCSA. Download it at safer.fmcsa.dot.gov, company snapshot, print. It shows you are a legitimate carrier with an active license.

Fourth, the broker-carrier agreement. The contract between you and the broker. Read it carefully before signing. Pay attention to payment terms, days to pay, detention policy, TONU policy, liability limits, and any exclusive use clause — some brokers prohibit you from working with competitors, and those are worth avoiding. If something is unclear, ask, or show it to a lawyer.

Fifth, the equipment list. Your equipment: truck type, year, VIN, trailer type, dimensions. The broker uses it to match you with suitable freight. Keep it updated.

Sixth, banking information or a factoring NOA. Payment details: bank name, routing number, account number. If you use factoring, send the NOA — the notice of assignment — from your factoring company. The NOA tells the broker to pay them, not you. Important: send banking information only to verified brokers.

Now, building relationships. Spot market freight off the load board is where you start. But the real money is in relationship freight — when a broker calls you directly with loads that never reach the board. Relationship freight pays ten to fifteen percent above spot, because the broker values reliability and will pay a premium for guaranteed service.

The first step is still the factoring check. Before investing in a relationship, make sure the broker is sound. Check them on your factoring company's site — thirty seconds. There is no point building a relationship with a broker who pays late or carries a poor credit rating. Your factoring company has already done that work; use their data.

How to become a preferred carrier. Deliver on time, every time. Report problems early — a delay, a breakdown — not at the last minute. Answer calls and messages quickly. Stay professional in how you communicate. And never cancel a load after booking; that destroys trust instantly. After fifty successful deliveries, brokers start offering you freight first.

The strategy: build relationships with ten to fifteen reliable brokers who give you loads regularly. That creates steady income without hunting the board every day. The formula: five large brokers for stability, five mid-sized for better rates, and three to five niche brokers for specialized freight. Once fifteen brokers know you by name, you are no longer dependent on the spot market. And all fifteen should be approved by your factoring company — that is your quality filter.

What to do after every delivery: send the POD fast, the same day. Then a short message: "Load delivered on time, no issues, POD attached, looking forward to the next one." Thirty seconds of work that reads as professionalism. Within a month, that broker calls you first.

Remember: brokers are your main business partners. Eighty percent of spot market freight moves through them. Your factoring company is your first and best tool for judging whether a broker is sound. Knowing how to spot fraud and how to build long-term relationships protects your business and grows your income.

Verify every broker. Build relationships with the reliable ones. Use factoring for protection. Do that, and you will succeed in this business.
