Six real paperwork mistakes that cost money. Every one of these is an actual loss.

Mistake one: not reading the rate con before signing. You agreed to $2,500 on the phone, but the rate con says $2,200. Or detention: zero dollars. Or "paperwork within 24 hours, otherwise 48." You signed it, so that is the law now. The broker will say: the document says $2,200 and you signed it. The fix: read every line. Check the rate, detention, TONU, payment terms, the paperwork deadline and the broker's MC number.

Mistake two: the driver does not inspect the freight at pickup. The BOL says twenty pallets, and two are damaged. The driver signs without looking. At delivery there are eighteen pallets and visible damage. The receiver notes shortage and damage. Now there is a $15,000 claim against you, because your driver signed a clean BOL. The fix: the driver counts the pallets and inspects the freight. Any discrepancy goes on the BOL, in writing: "eighteen pallets received, BOL says twenty." Or "visible damage on pallet number three."

Mistake three: sending the POD late. Delivered Monday, POD sent Friday. Days to pay — thirty days — starts when the broker receives the POD, so you just lost four days. Over a year that adds up to more than two hundred days of delayed payment. And with factoring, they will not buy the invoice without a POD at all. The fix: the driver photographs the BOL right after delivery, and you send POD plus invoice to the broker or factoring company the same day.

Mistake four: unreadable photos. The driver shot the BOL in the dark, at an angle, with a finger over the lens. Text unreadable, signature invisible. The broker rejects it: unreadable, please resubmit. Meanwhile the driver is five hundred miles away. The fix: good light, all four corners in frame, text readable, signature sharp. Check the photo immediately after taking it.

Mistake five: not recording detention time. The driver sat four hours at unloading. Detention is $75 an hour, so that is $150 for the two hours beyond free time. But there is no arrival timestamp, no photo, no record. The broker says: prove you waited four hours. You cannot. That is $150 gone. The fix: the driver photographs arrival with a timestamp, photographs the start of unloading, photographs departure, and pulls GPS data from the ELD. Then sends it to you immediately.

Mistake six: not keeping copies. Six months later the broker disputes payment: we never received the POD. You have no copy, the email is deleted, the file is gone. Now $2,500 is in question. By law you must keep these records at least three years. The fix: build a filing system. A folder on Google Drive or Dropbox for every load — load number, rate con, BOL, POD, invoice. Never delete them.
