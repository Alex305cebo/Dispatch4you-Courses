Amazon Relay for Dispatchers

Amazon hauls its own freight with other people’s trucks and hands those runs out through its own platform, Relay. There is no broker here, no haggling and no waiting for money, but there are rules that are not obvious to a newcomer. This four-part guide takes you from first contact to a dispatcher’s working week. Part one: what Relay is and how to get connected.

1. What Amazon Relay is, in plain words

What it is

Amazon sells goods online and moves them across the country itself. To do that it runs hundreds of facilities and owns more than eighty thousand trailers. What Amazon does not own in any real quantity is tractors with drivers. Those it hires from ordinary carriers, companies like yours. To hand those runs out, Amazon built its own platform, Amazon Relay: a website for the dispatcher and an app for the driver. Through them you get Amazon freight, and Amazon sees where your truck is.

How this differs from working with a broker

In the usual setup there is a middleman between you and the owner of the freight. The broker finds the load, haggles with you on the phone, emails a rate confirmation and pays a month later. In Relay there is no middleman. Amazon owns the freight, the price is already printed on the load card and is not negotiable, booking is one button, and the money comes every week.

Who is who here

  • Amazon owns the freight and the trailers. It talks to you through the site, the app and support.
  • The carrier is your company with its own MC and DOT numbers. The company registers in Relay, not the dispatcher and not the driver.
  • The dispatcher works on the Relay site: picks loads, assigns drivers, watches the run, files support cases.
  • The driver works in the Relay app on his phone: sees the trip, taps arrival and departure, gets through the gate.

What a normal Amazon run looks like

The driver shows up at an Amazon facility bobtail, with no trailer. He hooks a preloaded Amazon trailer, hauls it to another Amazon facility, drops it there and, if there is another leg, hooks the next one. This is called power only: you supply the tractor and the driver, Amazon supplies the trailer and the freight. Most Relay work is exactly this. Live loading, where the driver waits at a door while the trailer is loaded, happens too, just less often.

The kinds of facilities

Every Amazon facility has a code made of three letters and a number. The letters are the nearest major airport code, the number is which facility it is in that area. ONT8 is the eighth facility near Ontario airport in California. JFK8 is the eighth near JFK in New York. FTW1 is the first near Fort Worth in Texas. Reading the code on a load card tells you the region before you even open the address: a run from CDW5 to JFK8 is New Jersey to New York, a short leg over toll bridges.

  • Fulfillment center (FC in Amazon's codes). Goods are stored here and orders are picked. Huge yards, and usually drop-and-hook: drop what you brought, hook the next one.
  • Sortation center (SC). Finished parcels are sorted by delivery area. The work runs in timed waves, so being thirty minutes late can blow a wave.
  • Delivery station (DS). Parcels leave here on small vans to customers. Tight yards, unloading often at night.
Remember Relay is Amazon without a middleman: fixed price, one-button booking, money every Friday. The dispatcher works on the site, the driver in the app. Most of the time you pull Amazon trailers with your own tractor between Amazon facilities.

2. How a company gets into Relay

What it is

Registration is free and happens once for the whole company. You apply at relay.amazon.com, the form takes about twenty minutes and the answer comes in one to three weeks. The check is automatic: Amazon pulls your record from the federal regulator's databases and reads your certificate of insurance. If a single item fails, the application is rejected with no explanation. So you check the company against the list below before applying, not after.

What Amazon checks

ItemRequirementIn plain words
Age of the authorityInterstate operating authority active for at least 180 daysA brand-new company will not be accepted. Apply in month three and you get an automatic rejection.
Entity typeListed with FMCSA as a for-hire property carrierIf you are listed as a broker or as a private carrier hauling your own goods, you are out. Fixed with an MCS-150 update.
Safety ratingSatisfactory, None or Not RatedA Conditional or Unsatisfactory rating after an audit closes the door until the rating is revised.
CSA BASIC percentilesUnsafe driving and hours of service under 60%, vehicle maintenance, controlled substances and driver fitness under 75%These are the CSA numbers. Amazon's thresholds are stricter than the alert levels the regulator uses.
Violation rate on inspectionsDriver violations no more than 35%, vehicle violations no more than 50%. Counted after three inspections in twelve monthsNew for 2026. There is no appeal. Two dirty inspections out of three and you are cut off, even with clean CSA numbers.
EquipmentDay cab or sleeper; 48' or 53' dry van; 53' reefer; 16' or 26' box truck at dock heightThere is no minimum fleet size, one tractor is enough. For power only you need no trailer at all.

Insurance: where most applications die

Nobody asks you for the certificate. You give Amazon your insurance agent's contact details and Amazon's vendor requests the document directly. If your agent takes a week to answer, the application sits for a week. Call the agent yourself the day you apply.

  • Auto liability: $1,000,000. Everybody has it.
  • Trailer interchange: $50,000 as its own line. You will be pulling Amazon trailers and Amazon wants to see exactly that line. Most policies default to $25,000 or carry no such line, and the system rejects the application silently. the number one reason for rejection
  • General liability: $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate. Owner-operators often do not carry it and have to add it.
  • Cargo: $100,000. Standard.
  • Workers' compensation: per state rules if you have W-2 drivers.

Check your own company

Tick what is already true. The verdict updates as you go.

From real life A carrier applied with an ordinary policy that covered a non-owned trailer at $25,000. Two weeks later the rejection arrived with no reason given. The dispatcher went down the list, found that line, had the agent raise it to $50,000 and send a fresh certificate to Amazon's vendor. The company was approved eight days later. Checking before applying would have saved three weeks.
Important: portal access Relay has three kinds of user. Primary administrator is the owner: banking, tax forms, granting access. Administrator books loads, assigns drivers, adds drivers and files disputes. And the role literally called Dispatcher can only look. It cannot book and it cannot assign a driver. Ask the owner for Administrator access from day one, otherwise you will be staring at a $3,000 load unable to press the button.
Remember The authority must be older than 180 days, the safety rating must not be Conditional, and the insurance certificate must carry a separate $50,000 trailer interchange line. Expect an answer in one to three weeks. The dispatcher needs the Administrator role, not the role named "Dispatcher".

3. How a driver joins Relay

What it is

A driver cannot sign himself up. The dispatcher invites him from the company portal, then he installs the Amazon Relay app and passes the checks. Until the checks clear he cannot be put on a trip. From invite to ready takes two to five business days, which is why a new driver is onboarded well before his first load, not on the day of it.

Step by step

The dispatcher sends the invite

In the portal: Manage Resources, Driver Roster, Add Driver. You need his name, phone, email, license number and state. He gets a text with a link to the app.

The driver proves who he is

In the app he takes a selfie and photographs both sides of his license. That check takes about forty-eight hours. Then comes the background check: criminal history and motor vehicle record, using his SSN or ITIN and personal email. It has been mandatory for every new driver since August 2025.

He watches the training videos

Short Amazon clips about gates, yards and handling trailers. Until they are watched the app will not let him start a trip. The dispatcher can see in the roster whether training is done.

Phone settings

The step everyone skips, and the one that matters most. Location for the app must be set to Always, not "While using". Notifications on, battery saver off for the Relay app. Without this Amazon cannot see your truck on the road and your score starts dropping from the very first trip.

What the driver sees in the app

The app shows his trips, addresses, times and trailer number, and walks him through the steps: check in, pick up, drop off. He cannot see what the load pays and he cannot book anything. All of that is the dispatcher's job on the site. What he does get is truck-legal navigation to the exact door, weather along the route and a chat with support.

Is this driver ready for his first trip?

From real life A driver was invited on Thursday and put on his first load on Friday evening. The identity check was still running and his status was Pending, so the app refused to start the trip. The load had to be dropped and the company took its first score penalty for a rejection. The rule is simple: a driver in Pending never goes on a trip.
Remember The dispatcher invites the driver and the checks take two to five days. Location Always, battery saver off. The driver never sees the rate and never books. Only a driver in Active status goes on a trip.
